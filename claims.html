First Steps

How to File an
Insurance Claim

Filing a commercial property claim can feel overwhelming. Our team simplifies the process by acting as your advocate from day one. Whether it's a burst pipe, fire damage, or storm-related loss, here is what you need to do immediately.

1

Mitigate Further Damage

Take reasonable steps to prevent additional loss, such as tarping a roof or shutting off water. Save your receipts for these emergency repairs.

2

Document Everything

Take clear, time-stamped photos and videos of all damage before any cleanup begins. Do not throw away damaged items until instructed.

3

Contact SecureBiz Immediately

Call our 24/7 hotline. We will notify the carrier, dispatch a field adjuster, and assign a dedicated claims advocate to your file.

How long does a commercial property claim take?

The timeline varies based on complexity. Simple claims (e.g., minor water damage) can settle in 2-4 weeks. Complex catastrophic losses (e.g., fire, hurricane) can take 6-12 months to fully resolve, especially if business interruption coverage is involved.

What is the difference between ACV and RCV?

ACV (Actual Cash Value) pays replacement cost minus depreciation. RCV (Replacement Cost Value) pays the full cost to replace the damaged property with new, similar items, without deducting depreciation. We highly recommend RCV policies for commercial real estate.

Does my policy cover mold remediation?

Standard policies often have limited mold coverage (e.g., $10,000). If mold results from a covered peril (like a burst pipe), the remediation may be covered up to the sub-limit. We can help you secure a mold endorsement to increase this limit.

Can I choose my own contractor for repairs?

Yes, you generally have the right to choose your contractor. However, the insurance carrier will only pay the "reasonable and customary" market rate for repairs. Our claims advocates help negotiate between your contractor and the adjuster to ensure fair pricing.

What if my claim is denied?

If a claim is denied, we review the carrier's rationale against the specific policy language. If we believe the denial is unjustified, we can file a formal appeal or, in some cases, engage a public adjuster or legal counsel to dispute the decision.
